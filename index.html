Md Abdullah-Al Kaiser

I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Electrical and Computer Engineering department at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, under the supervision of Prof. Akhilesh Jaiswal. My research focuses on analog and mixed-signal circuit design, in-memory and in-sensor computing, photonic computing, neuromorphic computing, and device-circuit-algorithm codesign.

Previously, I earned my M.S. from the Ming Hsieh Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering in 2022 at the University of Southern California (USC), and my B.Sc. degree in Electrical and Electronic Engineering from the Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology (BUET), where I graduated in the top 5% of my class.

During my Ph.D. tenure, I was honored with several prestigious recognitions, including the DATE Travel Grant Award (2025) and DAC Young Fellow Award (2024). I also received multiple teaching accolades at USC, such as the Jenny Wang Excellence in Teaching Award (2024) and Charles Weber Teaching Award (2023), alongside the USC Annenberg Graduate Fellowship (2019–2023).

I have authored 20+ peer-reviewed publications in leading venues such as the Design Automation Conference (DAC), npj Unconventional Computing, npj 2D Materials and Applications, Frontiers, ICASSP, GLVLSI, and others.

My industry experience includes internships at Apple Inc. and Meta Reality Labs. At Apple, I worked on mixed-signal circuit design, MATLAB-based hardware modeling, and verification within the AMS team. At Meta, I focused on display pixel design, including schematic/layout work and physical verification (DRC, LVS, PEX).
