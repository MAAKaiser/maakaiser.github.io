Research

Research Interests

  • Analog and Mixed-Signal Circuit Design
  • In-memory and In-sensor Computing
  • Photonic Computing

My research focuses on the co-design of hardware and algorithms for energy-efficient and scalable computing systems. I am particularly interested in advancing edge intelligence using emerging memory and photonic technologies, with emphasis on system-level integration of processing-in-memory and in-sensor compute paradigms.

Neuromorphic-In-Pixel Computing

Modern vision systems use separate sensors and processors, causing energy and latency overheads that hinder real-time performance and scalability, especially in edge AI with tight power and area constraints.

We propose a hybrid CMOS+X neuromorphic processing-in-pixel-in-memory architecture integrating domain-wall magnetic tunnel junctions (MDW-MTJs) with CMOS transistors. This enables massively parallel, asynchronous in-pixel spiking CNN processing via event-driven multiply-accumulate operations. Weights are encoded using MDW-MTJ resistance states and transistor geometry, allowing programmable thresholds, long integration times, and energy-efficient computation. A 3D-stacked layout with fine-pitch Cu-Cu bonding achieves dense vertical integration without area overhead.

This architecture offers scalable and energy-efficient in-sensor computing with performance approaching state-of-the-art neuromorphic benchmarks, while lowering backend energy requirements by upwards of 45%.

For more details on the architecture, implementation, and evaluations, please refer to our published papers in Frontiers (2023), DAC (2024), and ICASSP (2024).

Photonic In-Memory Computing

Traditional von Neumann architectures face fundamental challenges due to continuous data movement between memory and processing units, leading to significant latency and power inefficiencies. These issues worsen with technology scaling, as electrical interconnect resistance and capacitance increase, constraining the performance and energy efficiency needed for emerging AI, IoT, and ML applications.

To address these limitations, we propose a novel differential photonic SRAM (pSRAM) bitcell-augmented, scalable mixed-signal multi-bit photonic tensor core. This architecture integrates electro-optic memory with ultra-fast matrix multiplication capabilities using fabrication-friendly integrated photonic components. Complementing this, our innovative 1-hot encoding electrooptic analog-to-digital converter (eoADC) converts analog photonic multiplication outputs into digital bitstreams, enabling efficient processing in the electrical domain.

Implemented using GlobalFoundries’ monolithic 45SPCLO technology, our photonic tensor core achieves computation speeds of 4.10 tera-operations per second (TOPS) with power efficiency of 3.02 TOPS/W. The eoADC operates at 8 GS/s while consuming only 2.32 pJ per conversion, and the pSRAM bitcell supports memory updates at 20 GHz, facilitating high-throughput, energy-efficient processing for large-scale datasets.

For a deeper dive into the design, validations, and detailed performance metrics, please refer to our publications in DAC (2025), ASAP (2025), and arXiv (2025).
